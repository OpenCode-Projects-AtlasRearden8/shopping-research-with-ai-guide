# Chapter 5 · When to buy (and when to just buy)

There's a sweet spot between two expensive mistakes: panic-buying at full price on a Tuesday, and waiting seven months for a sale that saves you $60 while the thing you needed wasn't in your life for the whole time. This chapter is about finding the middle, mostly with a calendar and a little patience.

## Step one: learn the usual price

The single most useful timing habit costs nothing: **track the item for 30 days before buying.** Price-history sites (search for one named after a famous desert mammal, and similar) plot an item's price over time, and they change how sales look:

- A price that never moves except for the odd coupon? You're not missing much by waiting, or by not waiting.
- A "50% off!" flash deal that sits above the item's average price? That's a **price anchor**, a fictional "was" price designed to make the today-price look virtuous (see [glossary](glossary.html)).
- A price that dips predictably? Predictability is exactly the thing you exploit.

Also open the listing on two other reputable retailers before buying. Cross-checking prices across stores is the poor man's price-tracking, and it catches fake "sales" instantly.

## The category calendars

Sale seasons are real, predictable, and category-specific. Here's the cheat sheet:

| Category | Good windows | Why those windows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Laptops | Back-to-school (late summer), Black Friday season, and the weeks right after new chip generations launch | Schools buy in bulk then; old models get cleared to make shelf room |
| Phones | 4 to 8 weeks after the big September launches, and carrier promo cycles right after | New flagships launch, and last year's model instantly becomes "old news"; the discount is real and quick |
| Appliances | Late in the year as next year's models land, plus big holiday weekends and rebate cycles (check your utility company) | Turnover clears floor stock; rebates stack onto sale prices |
| Mattresses | Any long weekend honestly | Mattress "sales" are a permanent condition; don't stall months for a banner |

Notice the phones line carefully: **the previous flagship** in month five after a launch is often the best phone deal in tech, period. It loses feature-flag status and nothing else.

## Wait or buy? The two-question test

Run these before you decide to camp on a listing:

1. **Is a known sale window within two weeks?** If yes, wait. If it's more than a month away, the expected savings rarely survive the waiting cost (the "what is my time and sleep worth" clause).
2. **Is this model about to be replaced?** If a successor is announced or next year's model is imminent, either wait for the new one to push down old stock, or jump immediately onto the outgoing model while everything's discounted. Both end well; dithering between the two does not.

If the answers are "no" and "no," the best price is today's price, and buying today buys you months of actually using the thing. That trade is almost always worth it.

## Open-box and refurbished: the good kind of second-hand

Floor models, returns, and display units can be 15 to 40% off for products that are physically fine. The safe way in:

- **Buy from the seller that made or certifies it.** "Refurbished" means most when the manufacturer or an authorized shop sells it, tests it, and stands behind it with a warranty. Marketplace-seller "refurb" with no warranty is a gamble with your money as the stake.
- **Mind the return window anyway.** Open-box is where testing matters most: dead pixels, dents, missing cables. Test thoroughly in week one, while returning is still free.
- **For appliances, ask about any water damage, loose hoses, or install kit mishaps before you accept the box.** Returns of plug-in items are usually fine to inspect at home; hardwired installs deserve a careful look first.

## A short note on deadlines

If the old thing died yesterday and you need a replacement tomorrow, give the tracking a miss and buy the best thing you can afford today, from a retailer with a strong return window. Return policies are a time machine: buy now, keep watching prices, and if the price drops hard next week, refund the difference if the retailer allows it, or return-and-rebuy where allowed. Not elegant, but effective.

And if you're the opposite kind of shopper, the kind who waits every year for Black Friday, one gentle warning: the best deals of that season are genuinely good, but the *loudest advertised* ones are often mid. Your [Chapter 3 audit](spot-fakes.html) skills apply to ads too.
