# Chapter 4 · Spec sheets, translated

A spec sheet is the manufacturer's menu of exact, checkable numbers, and it's the single most trustworthy document in shopping. It's also written by engineers for engineers, which means it assumes words like "nits" and "MT/s" are self-explanatory. They aren't, and this chapter is the translation.

One principle before the tables: **the three-numbers rule.** For any category, about three numbers decide whether you'll love the thing in daily life. The remaining forty spec lines are engineering trivia you'll never think about again. Find your three (your criteria list from [Chapter 1](criteria.html) points at them) and let the rest go.

Also, a habit worth adopting right now: when a claim matters, read it on the **manufacturer's official product page**, not a retailer bullet list. Retailer bullets paraphrase, drop units, and occasionally interpolate ("up to 8 hours!" becomes "8 hours" becomes "all-day"). The official page is where numbers have context.

## Laptops

| Spec line | Plain English | What matters for most people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPU model name | The processor's family, generation, and tier, in code | The generation matters more than the number after it; a "5" of a new generation usually beats a "7" of an old one |
| 16 GB RAM | Working memory; how much can be open at once | 16 GB is the comfortable floor in 2026; 8 GB means slowdowns sooner than you'd like |
| Soldered RAM | RAM glued to the board; not upgradeable | A forever choice; if it's 16, fine; if it's 8, you're stuck |
| 1 TB SSD | Permanent file storage, fast kind | 512 GB is workable, 1 TB is comfy; treat slower "eMMC" storage as a red flag |
| 300 nits | Screen brightness unit | 250 nits works indoors only; 400+ survives a sunny window |
| 60/120 Hz | How many times per second the screen redraws | 60 is normal; 120 feels nicer when scrolling and matters for games |
| 52 Wh battery | Battery fuel tank size | Rough guide to hours, but the chip and screen brightness drink it down; reviews give real numbers |
| Thunderbolt / USB-C / USB-A | Plug shapes and what they can do | USB-C is the shape; Thunderbolt is a speed class inside USB-C that not every C port has |
| 1.2 kg weight | What your shoulders feel at hour two | The difference between 1.2 and 1.7 kg is always bigger than it reads on paper |

The most expensive hidden spec in laptops is **repairability**: glued batteries, soldered RAM, and proprietary parts turn an "affordable" repair into a "buy another one" repair. If that's on your MUST list, look for teardown mentions in expert reviews.

## Phones

| Spec line | Plain English | What matters for most people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chipset name | The phone's brain, with a generation and tier | Midrange chips of this year beat flagship chips of three years ago at half the price |
| 8/12/16 GB RAM | Working memory | Far less important than marketing suggests; phones manage memory gracefully, so bigger numbers matter less than on laptops |
| 128/256/512 GB storage | Room for photos, apps, videos | Photos eat storage; 128 GB is the practical floor, 256 GB is the stress-free choice |
| 200 MP camera | Megapixels, i.e. picture blob count | Mostly a banner number; sensor size and stabilization shape real photos much more |
| 7 years of updates | How long the maker promises OS and security fixes | The best spec nobody bragged about until recently; long support keeps the phone safe and current for years past the banner ads |
| IP68 | Dust-tight, can survive dunking | "6" blocks dust, "8" (the second digit) covers water; "IPX7" has no dust rating at all |
| 5000 mAh battery | Fuel tank, phone-sized | Numbers alone mislead; chip efficiency decides whether you wake up at 60% or 90% |
| eSIM | A SIM card, as software | No physical tray; great for travel profiles, annoying if your carrier still mails plastic |

Camera math, one more time, because it's the most misread line in phones: megapixels are how many dots, sensor size is how much light each setup catches, stabilization is whether your hands ruin it. A big lens on an old cheap phone beats a "200 MP" label on a new one every time.

## Appliances

| Spec line | Plain English | What matters for most people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 place settings | Dishwasher capacity, measured in a formal place-setting unit | Real-world proxy: counts plates, cups, cutlery sets per load, not pots and pans |
| 8 kg wash load | Washing machine drum capacity | 7 to 9 kg suits most households; bigger drums matter when you wash duvets |
| 42 dB | Noise level; 30 is a library, 50 is a quiet conversation | Under ~45 dB for bedrooms and open kitchens; you'll hear this number every single night |
| Inverter compressor | Motor that varies its speed instead of on/off | Quieter, steadier, uses less power; worth the small premium in fridges and HVAC |
| ENERGY STAR | Third-party certification for efficiency | A floor, not a ceiling; check the actual yearly kWh estimate next to it |
| Wi-Fi enabled | The appliance talks to your phone | Ask what you'd actually do with the app; a filter reminder is genuinely useful, "preheating the toaster" is a demo |
| Warranty, parts availability | What the maker guarantees to fix, and with what | A 10-year warranty on a part with a 9-month repair backlog isn't 10 years of peace |
| Consumables | Filters, bags, rinse aid, calibrations | Add up a year of the stuff the machine eats; it's real money hiding in the manual |
| 178 cm depth, 90 cm with doors | Physical size including the part that moves | Measure the alcove, the doorway, the hallway corner, and the stairwell; handles swing |

Appliances marry you before a first date, so measure twice. The most common "it doesn't fit" story is the fridge that fit the alcove but not the hallway bend, doors off and at an angle, two friends and a doorframe casualty.

## Mattresses

| Spec line | Plain English | What matters for most people |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Firmness 6/10 | Soft-to-firm score, brand's own scale | The scales are not standardized; one brand's 6 is another's 4; trust trials and reviews over the number |
| Memory foam | Slow, contouring foam that hugs and sleeps warm | Great pressure relief, poor airflow; hot sleepers, note |
| Latex | Springy, bouncy, breathable foam | Cooler and longer-lasting than memory foam, heavier and pricier |
| Pocketed coils | Springs, individually wrapped fabric sleeves | Move independently, so partner tossing transmits less; usually the best of both worlds with foam on top |
| Edge support | Whether the perimeter holds you up | Matters if you sit on the side of the bed or sleep near the edge |
| 365-night trial | Test duration for returns | Read the fine print: return fee? pickup at your door? exchange-only for a different firmness? |
| Off-gassing | New-foam smell, from packaging chemistry | Strongest the first 48 hours; airing the room speeds it up; certifications like CertiPUR-US bound the chemicals used |
| Motion transfer | How much one sleeper's roll shakes the other | Foam beds excel; old-school springs, worst; coil-plus-foam hybrids, decent |
| Indentation warranty clause | Warranty trigger for "sagging" | Usually only covers dips deeper than about 1.5 inches (and needs photos from day one) |

The truth about mattresses: **you cannot buy one with your brain.** Your body is the only instrument that measures you. This is why the trial period is the single most important mattress spec, and why the fine print on returns beats the fine print on foam layers. Buy from a place that lets you back out cheaply, then actually test the return window instead of feeling bad about using it.

## "Up to" theater

`Up to 12 hours of battery` means a lab washes its hands of real life. `Up to 45 dB` often means the quietest mode it has ever achieved once. When you see "up to," mentally read "rarely." When you see "as low as," read "once." Real-world numbers come from review measurements, which is where the loops of this manual ([Chapter 2](ai-reviews.html) to collect, [Chapter 3](spot-fakes.html) to audit) earn their keep.
